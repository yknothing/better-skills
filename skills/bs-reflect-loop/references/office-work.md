# Office Work Reflection Lens

Use this lens for meetings, decisions, plans, documents, communication, collaboration, vendor work, operations, and handoffs.

## Separate Activity From Outcome

Common office artifacts prove narrower things than people assume:

- a meeting happened; it does not prove a decision was made;
- a decision was discussed; it does not prove authority accepted it;
- a task was assigned; it does not prove ownership, capacity, or commitment;
- a document was delivered; it does not prove it was understood or used;
- a message was sent; it does not prove the recipient received the intended meaning;
- a deadline was recorded; it does not prove dependencies can support it.

Reflect on the observable outcome, not the volume of activity.

## Work Lenses

### Meetings

- What decision, alignment, discovery, or coordination outcome justified the meeting?
- Which statements were proposals, decisions, objections, or unresolved questions?
- Did the right decision-makers and evidence enter at the right time?
- What should change in the next agenda, pre-read, participant list, or decision record?

Do not invent consensus, owners, or due dates from conversational tone.

### Decisions

- What was selected, by whom, and under which constraints?
- Which alternatives were seriously considered?
- What evidence or assumption carried the decision?
- What future signal should trigger review or reversal?

If no authorized decision was made, record the state as a proposal or open choice.

### Documents

- Who needed to decide or act after reading?
- Was the document optimized for that task and audience?
- Which revision changed comprehension or action rather than surface polish?
- Was completion measured by file delivery or by downstream use?

Route prose-quality work to the writing Skill; use Reflect Loop for what the document process teaches future work.

### Planning and execution

- Was the outcome, owner, dependency, and completion evidence explicit?
- Which estimate or assumption caused the largest divergence?
- Did urgent work displace important work, and was that trade-off visible?
- Which checkpoint would have exposed the divergence sooner?

### Communication and collaboration

- Which context was shared, assumed, or lost?
- Did channel, timing, audience, or language affect the outcome?
- Was disagreement surfaced early enough to improve the work?
- Is the lesson about a person, a relationship, or a repeatable coordination mechanism?

Avoid personality diagnoses. Prefer observable interaction patterns and proportionate process changes.

Before persisting office evidence, classify its sensitivity and intended readership. Vendor bids, personal details, legal comments, private messages, security information, and candid interview material are Restricted by default. Preserve only the minimum necessary evidence, link to an authorized restricted source instead of copying it, and require explicit confirmation before any write that broadens access.

### Handoffs

- What artifact, decision state, owner, and next action crossed the boundary?
- What did the receiver need to reconstruct?
- Which acceptance or read-back step was missing?
- What is the smallest reusable handoff improvement?

## Useful Destinations

- A consequential choice may belong in a decision log.
- A repeatable meeting or handoff improvement may belong in a template or checklist.
- A local vendor or project lesson may belong in a project learning record.
- A durable organizational mechanism may belong in governance only after evidence and authority justify the promotion.

When the office workspace has no established knowledge surface, use the blank-space choices in `deposition-routing.md`; do not impose a software-repository taxonomy on it.
